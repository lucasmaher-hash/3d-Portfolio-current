Overview / Concept

1

Vacuum-sealed packaging is usually photographed flat — real-world gravity and suction don't leave many options. That constraint was part of why this felt worth building: in CGI the pack can stand upright, rotate freely, and be lit from any angle. The object itself was a deliberate choice too, inspired by intranetgirl's tutorial — I wanted something unconventional, a pairing you wouldn't normally see merchandised. A pharmaceutical vial sealed inside a zip bag ticks that box. It also made for a useful technical challenge: two materials with opposite physical behaviour, which pushed me into cloth simulation, Cycles rendering, and transparent shading for the first time.

Process

2
Modeling

The vial body modeled from a single profile, lathed and edge-loop-tightened around the cap. Cap separated as its own object so it could be UV-unwrapped cleanly for the label band. Subdivision surface kept on for the final render to round the silhouette without losing the printed-grade ribs at the base.

Topology

Bag subdivided to ~250k tris — enough resolution for the cloth sim to resolve fine wrinkles where the plastic suctions onto the bottle without exploding into noise.

Vacuum Sim

Cloth simulation with negative pressure — the bag actively sucks down onto the rigid bottle. 192 baked frames, with frame 90 picked as the hero.

Shading

Bag mixes Transparent BSDF for clarity, Translucent BSDF for the milky scatter on the bunched areas, and Glossy with IOR-driven Fresnel for the wet plastic highlights.

Final Render

Cycles output at 1920×1400, single key light at 6500K, denoised in compositor. Two hero passes shipped — a cool blue clinical front-on, and a rose-tinted angle used as the portfolio cover.
