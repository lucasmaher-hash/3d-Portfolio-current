Cybercoffee

At a Glance

Coffee machines have some of the most forgettable interfaces in the world. I wanted to push that mundanity somewhere visually specific — a retro-futuristic interface controlled entirely by six physical side buttons, with CRT scanlines and a Digital-7 readout pulled straight from a 2003 sci-fi film. Designed in Figma, rebuilt pixel-perfect in HTML, then modeled into a full 3D machine in Blender so it could actually sit on a counter.

Design process

1

Coffee-machine interfaces are one of the most overlooked corners of product design — nobody expects them to be interesting, which is exactly why I wanted to make one that is. Brushed aluminium, a stark digital typeface, and a CRT-era colour palette give it a distinctly retro feel, but the goal was never nostalgia for its own sake — it's a machine that's meant to read as familiar and futuristic at the same time.

A full turn around the finished machine — interface and body as one object. Even the renders lean into the era: a soft cream-and-blue backdrop and a touch of film grain, so the presentation stays as in-period as the machine itself.

Materials stay deliberately minimal: brushed aluminium for the body, white plastic for the buttons and handles, with a hint of red on the interface controls to match the display's own black-and-red palette.

Every one of the six side buttons maps to a single dedicated action — the whole point was to design out arrow keys and confirm presses entirely, so each click simply moves you to what's next.

Final result

2

Boot it up and you land on an always-on display showing what's left in the beans, water, and milk. Press any button and you're straight into the drink menu — every screen here runs on one rule: each of the six side buttons does a single dedicated thing, so a press always moves you forward, with no arrow keys or confirm button anywhere in the system. From there, drop into a saved preset or configure a drink from scratch — milk, water, beans — tracking your progress along the top bar, and saving the setup as a new preset while it brews. A few details reward using it for real: the side chevrons light up red the moment a button becomes actionable, a wrong press shakes the display instead of just sitting there, and you can set a wake-up timer for the first cup tomorrow morning.
