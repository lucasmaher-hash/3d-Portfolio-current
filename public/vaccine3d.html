Overview / Concept

0

A vacuum-sealed pharmaceutical pack rendered for the fictional brand S4AD — Software for Artists Day, a satirical capsule positioned at the intersection of technologists and artists. The study explores realistic plastic deformation, glass dispersion, and printed label artwork — aiming for product-photo plausibility, not the "obviously a render" look.

Process

5 STEPS  ·  MODELING → RENDER

Modeling

1

The vial body modeled from a single profile, lathed and edge-loop-tightened around the cap. Cap separated as its own object so it could be UV-unwrapped cleanly for the label band. Subdivision surface kept on for the final render to round the silhouette without losing the printed-grade ribs at the base.

Topology

2

Bag subdivided to ~250k tris — enough resolution for the cloth sim to resolve fine wrinkles where the plastic suctions onto the bottle without exploding into noise.

Vacuum Sim

3

Cloth simulation with negative pressure — the bag actively sucks down onto the rigid bottle. 192 baked frames, with frame 90 picked as the hero. The hang-tab punch is masked out of the sim so the top stays flat and printable, while the body collapses into those tight star-shaped wrinkles around the vial.

Shading

4

Bag mixes Transparent BSDF for clarity, Translucent BSDF for the milky scatter on the bunched areas, and Glossy with IOR-driven Fresnel for the wet plastic highlights. Blue tint baked into the zip seam reads as printed PVC.

Final Render

5

Cycles output at 1920×1400, single key light at 6500K, denoised in compositor. Two hero passes shipped — a cool blue clinical front-on, and a rose-tinted angle used as the portfolio cover.
