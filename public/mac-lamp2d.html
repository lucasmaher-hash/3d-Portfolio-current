Apple's early-2000s design language — translucent coloured plastic, playful forms, hardware you wanted to look at — has mostly disappeared from the company's lineup. This project brought some of that energy back, using a broken 2012 MacBook heading for a drawer.
The MacBook was deconstructed and its shell combined with a 3D-printed housing in translucent orange PLA — chosen to echo the colourful, see-through aesthetic of that era.
It was also a first exercise in precision CAD work — modeling to real measurements so that printed parts would fit salvaged hardware without play or gap.
